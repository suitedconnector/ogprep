# Utah Water — Cedar Valley (Iron County)

The short version: **in Utah you need the state's permission to use groundwater, and around
Cedar City the state stopped issuing it.** So the question on any parcel here is not "how deep
is the water" — it is "am I allowed to touch it at all."

---

## Why the basin is closed

- Cedar City Valley became a **critical management area** on **11 January 2021**.
- Safe yield **21,000 acre-feet/year**. Actual depletion **28,000**. Must come down by **7,000**.
- If every approved right were fully used, depletion would be **~50,000 af** — paper rights are
  about **2.4×** the water that actually exists.
- Officially **Water Right Area 73**. Right numbers starting `73-` are in this basin.

[Groundwater Management Plan (PDF)](https://www.waterrights.utah.gov/groundwater/ManagementReports/CedarValley/CedarCityValleyGWMP20210111.pdf)

## The regulation schedule — a priority date is a countdown

Rights get shut off **newest first**. Older beats newer, always.

| Phase | Date | Rights junior to | Acre-feet cut |
| ----- | ---- | ---------------- | ------------- |
| 1 | Jan 2035 | 31 Dec 1957 | 5,434 |
| 2 | Jan 2050 | 31 Dec 1954 | 7,330 |
| 3 | Jan 2060 | 31 Dec 1951 | 8,814 |
| 4 | Jan 2070 | 31 Dec 1935 | 6,761 |
| 5 | Jan 2080 | 25 Jul 1934 | 1,518 |

**A 2022-priority right here has ~13 years left. A pre-1935 right runs to 2070.**
Two rights with identical acre-feet can be worth wildly different money. No listing site shows this.

## Shares vs rights — different instruments

| | Water right | Company share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | Real property, state-recorded | Stock in a company that holds rights |
| Transfers via | Deed → county recording → Report of Conveyance → **change application** | Endorse certificate, company records it |
| State involvement | State Engineer must approve | **None** |
| Timeline | Months, approval not guaranteed | Days |
| Currently for sale in Area 73 | **Nothing** | Ask the company |

**This is why shares trade when rights don't.** No State Engineer approval needed.

Reference point: a South Jordan Canal share — $25 *par value* from 1880 — currently lists at
**$14,000**. Par value is a fixed accounting figure and means nothing; never anchor on it.

### Questions to ask any water company

1. **How many shares are outstanding?** → `125.62 af ÷ shares = acre-feet per share`.
   This is the only number that matters and it is **not public** — ownership ledgers are private.
2. What does one share deliver in a *dry* year, not an average one?
3. Does the delivery system physically reach the parcel? Whose cost to connect?
4. Annual assessments?
5. Must the board approve a transfer? Can they refuse?
6. **Are any of your wells being abandoned?** ← ask this one explicitly

## What a right costs, if you can find one

State standards for a household:

- **0.45 af** indoor domestic
- **0.028 af** per cow/horse
- **4.0 af** per irrigated acre

House + 2 animals + ⅛ acre garden ≈ **1.0 acre-foot** (the Division's own Cedar City example).

Observed listings Sept 2026: Area 54 at 0.75 af for $33,750; Area 15 at 0.336 af for $15,120.
Both ≈ **$45,000/acre-foot**. So the right alone ≈ **$45k** — more than the well, far more than
the land.

⚠️ Two sales, neither in Area 73. Order of magnitude only. **Currently zero Area 73 listings on
Utah Water Hub** — Kevin confirms owners are holding.

## Tax deed warning

Utah sells **tax deeds**, not liens (Utah Code 17-60-505). A tax deed is *"similar to a quit claim
deed"* (59-2-1351.1) — you get whatever the delinquent owner had, no warranty.

> **"A water right is not automatically attached to a property."**
> — Utah Division of Water Rights, *Buying, Selling & Transferring Water Rights*, 2024

If the right was **severed** — held separately, common in Utah — the tax deed reaches nothing.
The failure mode: a cheap Iron County parcel at tax sale, in a closed basin, water right severed
years ago. Dirt you cannot build on, and no route to water at any price.

**Check the Division's ownership records AND the county recorder's chain of title before bidding.**

## Abandoned wells still have owners

`Abandonded Well` (their spelling) in the type column describes the *point of diversion*, not the
right. Those rights came back **Perfected** or **Approved** — live.

- **Opportunity:** an owner with paper and no infrastructure has less reason to hold.
- **Risk:** seven years of non-use makes a right **subject to** forfeiture (Utah Code 73-1-4).
  Not automatic — needs a District Court declaration — but you could buy something a court voids.
- Ask: *when was water last beneficially used, and is a non-use application on file?*
